# Fabraka v3 — picture question research

Date: 2026-09-18. Scope: 32 real tools, in the fixed illustration order `fab3-001`–`fab3-032`.

All 12 previous picture questions and all 24 previous personal prompts were replaced with new IDs. The personal bank now contains 48 fictional situations; it makes no factual claims about players. Each personal prompt has one `{name}` and one `___`, an empty host-defined answer, and eight contextual suggestions. Eight suggestions leave spare false options when the host's answer and up to three aliases happen to match suggestions.

The picture questions describe documented uses of real objects. They use original Arabic paraphrases; no source photographs or illustrations were copied into the game. The image credit explicitly identifies the game images as AI-generated illustrations. Image production and visual acceptance are handled separately: a research citation confirms the tool's use, not the accuracy of a generated image.

## Decisions that avoid ambiguous answers

- **001 — darning mushroom:** it supports fabric during darning; it does not itself weave or sew. Both sock repair and fabric repair are accepted.
- **002, 003, 005, 027, 032 — textile tools:** these are distinct tools, despite related outputs. Lucet forms cord; the small tatting shuttle helps create knotted lace; the boat shuttle carries weft across a loom; a lace bobbin carries and manipulates thread; the hollow spool knitter produces a knitted cord. The questions identify the useful context without demanding obscure foreign tool names.
- **003 — tatting shuttle:** render the compact pointed shuttle. Do not substitute a large decorative knotting shuttle; historical knotting and tatting are not interchangeable.
- **004 — nostepinne:** the maker's documentation supports center-pull yarn balls. No unsupported invention date or national-origin claim is used.
- **007 — boot jack:** the notch grips the heel for removal. Do not render a shoehorn, which assists putting shoes on.
- **011 and 012 — candle tools:** 011 must be the bell/cone on a handle that extinguishes the flame. 012 must be scissors with a collection box that trim burnt wick. Historical “snuffers” may mean either object, so the Arabic prompts and descriptions distinguish them.
- **013 — wax seal:** the selected Getty instructions document a brass seal pressed into soft wax. The game does not assert a particular historic seal's ownership or authenticity.
- **014 — pounce pot:** museum documentation mentions both paper preparation and drying fresh ink. The prompt explicitly says “بعد كتابة رسالة بالحبر”. Paper preparation is deliberately not a false option. The explanation acknowledges the other documented use.
- **015 — bilboquet:** use the cup-and-ball form: a string connects the ball to the handled cup. The expected action is catching the tossed ball in the cup, not a different spike-and-hole variant.
- **016 — cup anemometer:** cup rotation measures wind speed, not wind direction. The direction answer is a deliberate false option.
- **017 — sundial:** the source describes a horizontal dial; the illustration should retain the plate, hour lines, and raised inclined gnomon. No calendar prediction or exact clock-reading challenge is implied.
- **018 — rain gauge:** the collected amount is read over a defined period. The tool does not forecast rain.
- **019 — tuning fork:** musician context restricts the question to pitch reference/tuning, avoiding ambiguity with medical or laboratory uses. The Smithsonian primary page's indexed text was readable; its direct reader fetch was intermittent.
- **020 — plumb bob:** a suspended weight supplies a vertical reference. The cited museum also discusses its use within a level; the standalone object's question asks about vertical alignment.
- **021 — proportional dividers:** the primary drafting manual, page 2-13 (PDF page 18), describes two legs on a sliding adjustable pivot. Moving the pivot establishes a ratio between the distances at their opposite ends; this transfers dimensions when enlarging or reducing drawings. The illustration must show two slotted crossed arms, one sliding pivot and four pointed tips, without a pencil or extra linkage. The [Smithsonian's proportional dividers collection](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object-groups/dividers-compasses/proportional-dividers) corroborates the construction and drawing use; its indexed text was readable, while direct reader fetches were intermittent. The government manual opened successfully and is the question's source.
- **022 — marking gauge:** the point marks at a fixed distance from an edge. The question is about marking, not measuring moisture, weight, or fastening boards.
- **023 — spokeshave:** the maker documents shaping wood and taking thin shavings. Debarking was removed from the false answers because a shaving tool may plausibly be used that way.
- **024 — wooden last:** this is the foot-shaped form used during shoemaking, not a shoe stretcher with an adjustable screw mechanism.
- **025 — sailmaker's palm:** the leather strap should include a thumb opening and an indented metal needle-pushing plate. Both pushing a needle through thick material and protecting the palm during sewing are accepted.
- **026 — fid:** the museum record documents strand separation for splicing and discusses traditional wood/bone and modern materials. The question avoids claiming that every fid is wooden.
- **028 — butter mold:** the Met documents the impressed butter design. [Illinois State Museum's education sheet, item 11](https://dnr.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/dnr/education/documents/prairiewhatisitactivity.pdf) additionally documents shaping butter and ejecting it with a decorative plunger. This is molding finished butter, not churning cream. The accepted illustration shows the decorative pattern on the separate handled plunger, so its description and explanation identify that piece without claiming the cup itself has an interior pattern.
- **029 — hand corn sheller:** the selected MIT D-Lab page links directly to [its background notes](https://d-lab.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/1%20CornSheller_BG.pdf), read during verification. They explain separating kernels from cobs and handheld ridged metal/plastic designs. The picture must show the inner ridges/teeth; removing outer corn leaves is not an accepted synonym. No MIT drawing was copied or adapted.
- **030 — pounce wheel:** Rose Brand's own product documentation identifies the perforation of patterns. The intended object is the craft tracing wheel, not a diagnostic sensory wheel. The accepted result is perforating/transferring a pattern, not cutting out a shape.
- **031 — bone folder:** the university preservation specialist documents crisp folds and flattening paper. The image description does not assert that the pictured material is animal bone.
- **032 — spool knitter:** Clover's own tutorial documents cord knitting and identifies its relation to the traditional spool knitter. The image may use the simple wooden cylinder rather than the brand's modern rotating product. The answer is a knitted cord, not a wound ball of yarn.
