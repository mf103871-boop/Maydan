# Final continuation review: cartoon and videogames

Reviewed 23 September 2026. This record covers these two packs only. It does not certify the unfinished whole bank or any generated artwork.

Both old files contained 240 questions. Each is now an explicit editorial selection of 40 questions, eight per tier, with IDs 901–908. The names, icons and cartoon `style: story` metadata are preserved. No shared index, status, manifest, game logic or media files were changed.

## Editorial work

- **cartoon:** 40 in-universe questions, zero production or performer questions, and eight topics. The selection covers familiar television characters plus animated film stories. Questions move from visible premises and household names to secondary characters, fictional places, particular objects and episode details. Twenty-nine answers are new to this pack; eleven match an old answer after Arabic normalization. Matching an answer does not mean copying the old stem.
- **videogames:** eight topics cover characters, resources, objectives, enemies, puzzles, movement, powers and worlds. Twenty-six answers are new to this pack; fourteen match an old answer. The selection replaces the old concentration on device trivia and English jargon with recognizable play: resources, movement, win conditions, character abilities and specific game locations.
- Each question was written explicitly; neither file was generated by taking the first eight old rows. All 480 old IDs are listed in the JSON companion as retired candidates. Existing central retirement ranges were left for the integrator.
- All targets are editorial estimates for Arabic families aged 16+: .80, .60, .40, .25 and .15. They are not measured correct-answer rates. The answer, rather than sentence length, guided tier placement. Examples: the flying house's balloons moved from old 400 to 200; the young explorer remains a less universal name at 600; familiar main-game identities precede particular laboratory and kingdom names.

## Source review

The companion JSON gives a supporting fact and precise primary-source URL for every one of the 80 questions. Reviewed sources include Paramount/Nickelodeon, Pixar, the official Smurfs and Popeye sites, Disney D23, Nintendo, SEGA, Mojang, The Pokémon Company, Valve, Riot, Epic, EA/PopCap, Supercell, Halfbrick, Rovio, Team Cherry, Studio MDHR and The Tetris Company.

Primary text was retrieved using web search excerpts and page opens. Some Nickelodeon profile opens redirected to regional homepages: those facts were checked against their indexed official profile text, and their evidence rows explicitly record that limitation. The redirected homepage was not treated as evidence for the missing profile. No fan wiki was used to establish a retained fact.

Examples of actual supporting details checked: Pixar identifies the Yeti's lemon snow cones, the rat clan's family members and the Axiom cleaning robot; Mojang explains both elytra repair and lingering-potion ingredients; the original Among Us role announcement gives the engineer's vent ability; Nintendo explains the shell's target and the hat capture mechanic. Stock links were not stamped onto unchecked rows.

Old questions with exaggerated or universal wording were not retained as-is. Examples include cartoon claims about what happens in “every” episode, the unsupported idea that all Minecraft objects are perfect cubes, the uncertain Sony workstation-name derivation, and the exaggerated claim that millions of E.T. copies were buried. The replacement Minecraft question asks about placing and breaking blocks, which the publisher's guide actually describes.
